# Hooks, shapes and the checklist

Read this before writing the first hook in a chat, and again before drafting a piece after a yes.

## One piece, three parts

| Part | Its job | In a video | In a post |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Stop the scroll and promise something | The first 1 to 5 seconds | The first one or two lines, before "see more" |
| Body | Deliver the promise | Most of the piece | The middle |
| Reward | Pay the reader for staying | The last 1 to 10 seconds | The last one to three lines |

If the body does not deliver what the hook promised, the piece fails, however good each part is alone.

## Pick one shape before drafting

- **List.** Items with no order. Put the number in the hook.
- **Steps.** Actions in a fixed order toward a goal. Put the number in the hook.
- **Story.** Events with a lesson. Beats: where you stood, what you wanted, what got in the way, the low point, the decision and what it cost, what changed, the lesson. Name what was at stake.

A tactical post reports what was actually done: the situation before, the actions, the result after. A reader should be able to copy it on Monday.

## The hook

A hook has two jobs:

- **Call out.** The right reader knows it is for them. Get this from the situation or the story, not from a label like "Founders:" at the front.
- **Value promise.** The reader knows what they get by staying.

Make it stronger by hitting several of these seven pulls: recency (it just happened), relevancy (it is about the reader's own problem), celebrity (a name or brand the reader already knows, used only with a real source), proximity (close to the reader's world), conflict (a fight, a mistake, a contrast), unusual (it breaks expectation), ongoing (part of a story that is still running). A post hook should hit at least two. A video's on-screen title should hit at least three.

Rules:

- Write three hooks. Keep the best. Put the other two in the draft notes.
- Open with the claim, not a question. Turn "Why does your CRM miss half your leads?" into "Your CRM misses half your leads."
- Skip filler openers such as "Here's how" and "Let me tell you".
- For a personal build or founder post, open with "I", a past-tense verb and a concrete result: "I cut my weekly report from four hours to ten minutes."
- Turn the stakes up as far as the truth goes. Name the thing the reader owns and make the problem physical. "Your suppression list has a gaping hole" beats "Your suppression list has a problem". Strong verbs work. Every claim still has to be true.

## Hook patterns to fill with your own facts

- Broken promise: "In March I promised myself I would not X. That lasted N days."
- Lessons from the other side: "N things my old job taught me about this job."
- One change: "I did X N times. Result Y. I changed one thing. Result Z." Do not overuse it.
- Dialogue: two lines of a real exchange that show a conflict.
- Funnel math: "Send N. Book 1. Take 5. Close 1."
- Milestone: "We just signed customer N." Only with a real number.
- Two futures: "I told the team they will either be replaced by X or become the 10x version of themselves."

Borrow the structure, never the words.

## Match the hook to who is reading

| Reader knows | Hook answers |
|---|---|
| Nothing about the problem | What the problem is |
| The problem | How to solve it |
| The kinds of solution | What the options are |
| Your brand | Why you are best |
| Your product | How to start |

## Links

On LinkedIn, keep links out of the post body. Say "link in the comments" and put the link in the first comment.

## Checklist before showing a draft

1. One shape, named.
2. A call out and a value promise, with no label before a colon.
3. At least two pulls hit (three for a video title), written down.
4. Three hooks written.
5. A story has a conflict, a decision and something at stake.
6. The reward pays the promise in plain words.
7. Every claim traces to something the user said or can show.
8. Nothing private, no rival named.
9. Read it aloud once. Cut every word that does no work.
