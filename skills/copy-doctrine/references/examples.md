# Before and after

Rules teach unevenly. Paired examples teach reliably.

## Jargon translated

> Before: Each signal carries a weight and a decay profile, summed across a rolling window.
> After: Every signal is worth points, and the points fade. Add them up and watch the total.

Same length. "Worth points, and the points fade" is what weight and decay mean.

> Before: Every identity is resolved and deduplicated on the identifiers you already hold.
> After: We match people on their email, phone and company domain. One person, one record, with no way to end up with two.

Three hard words gone, and the absolute claim survives.

## Redundancy cut

> Before: How well monitoring works depends on how much you can see. Every source you connect adds more. So there is no per-connection fee, no connector tier, and no cap, with unlimited connections on every plan.
> After: The more you connect, the better it gets. No per-connection fee and no cap, on any plan.

Forty-odd words to eighteen. The deleted list was drawn as a grid directly underneath.

> Before: One signal, one weight. A site visit is worth the same whichever tool spotted it. The score describes what the person did, not who saw it.
> After: One signal, one weight. A site visit is worth the same whichever tool spotted it.

Sentences two and three made the same claim.

## Tells removed

**Demonstrative opener plus a label**

> Before: Reading that pattern means holding every signal on one record and watching the total. That's a scoring problem, and alerts can't solve it.
> After: So you need every signal on one record. Some are worth more than others, and they fade at different speeds. Add up the points and watch the total move. An alert only tells you one thing happened.

"That's a scoring problem" labels what was just described.

**Vague pronoun**

> Before: Keep us running it, or bring it in-house whenever you want. Either way it is yours, and we put that in the contract on day one.
> After: Keep us running the system, or take it in-house whenever you want. You own it either way, and ownership is written into the contract on day one.

**Coined phrase**

> Before: Every fade rate is public.
> After: What every signal is worth, and how fast it fades, is public.

"Fade rate" is two common words and still means nothing, because nobody uses the phrase.

**Rhetorical question**

> Before: Don't see the signal you want? Bring your own.
> After: Bring your own signals.

**Imperative pair**

> Before: See the whole story on one record. Then know what to do about it.
> After: A buyer's whole story lands on one record. The score tells you who to call first.

**Fragment couplet**

> Before: We match on what you already have: no model, nothing to tune, nothing that drifts.
> After: We match on the email, phone and company you already have, so there is no model to tune and nothing to drift.

## Abstraction made concrete

> Before: No single signal can tell you when.
> After: No single signal tells you who is ready to buy.

"When" never said when what. The hedge "can tell" went too.

## A reply that echoed

> Before: Painful is the word, which is why the export tool keeps every field. Which part hurt most the last time you switched: the data or getting the team on board?
> After: Yeah, switching tools is painful, even when the new one is better. That's why the export keeps every field, so nothing gets left behind.

## A post that was too compressed

> Before: My reply bot's tag synced in as his status, and last write wins. The fix: tag first, write the status after, check every run.
> After: The AI that handles my replies tags anything it can't answer, and the tag was overwriting his status every time it synced. So now the tag goes on first and the real status gets written after it.

## Contrast kept, on purpose

> Most agents run on a schedule. Yours runs when a score crosses your line.

It looks like a banned contrast flip and is not. The reader does not know how agents are normally triggered, and the first sentence states a concrete fact. Without it, the second reads as a detail instead of an advantage.

## Stacking kept, on purpose

> Buying is a pattern, not one event. A visit, then a pricing page, then a new VP, then a reply. Any one of them means nothing. Together they are obvious.

Each sentence is a separate beat, not a link in a chain.

## Lines that never needed touching

> A clear no is worth more to both of us than a maybe.
> The second visit is the signal.
> Good writing on bad data is still wrong.

All plain, all carrying a point of view. Plain language applied too hard produces copy with no spine. Cut the tells and the jargon. Keep the judgement.
