# AI tells: the detection list

Two uses: things to avoid while drafting, and things to scan for when reviewing. Scan for them as strings, not by feel. Reading for tells catches most and misses the rest.

In cold email the tells are also a deliverability risk. Mailbox filters learn from what thousands of messages share, and near-identical smooth AI copy sent at volume is one of those patterns. A person should edit every cold email body, and the scan below should run on each one.

## Phrase level

| Tell | Why it reads as AI |
|---|---|
| Em dash | Sheer volume. The single loudest tell |
| Delve into, unpack | Nobody says "delve". A person says "look at" |
| This signals that, this underscores | Connects two ideas without an opinion about either |
| Navigate the complexities of, in an ever-changing landscape | Says nothing. What complexities, what is changing? |
| Synergies, leverage our learnings, holistic approach | Sounds profound, means nothing |
| It's worth noting, in conclusion, to summarize, in essence | Rarely appear in spoken English |
| As you may know, it's important to understand | Pre-explains the obvious |
| Excessive adverbs | "quietly underscores", "dramatically improves". Cut the adverb |
| Everything changed, everything shifted | Overwrought. Name what specifically changed |
| Forced negation | "not this but that", "instead of X, Y" |
| Rhetorical questions | "Ever wonder...", "What if...", "Sound familiar?" |
| Hedging stacks | "It's worth noting that, while X, you might want to consider Y" |
| Demonstrative openers | "That's a...", "This is...", "Here's why..." |

## Structural

- **A transition word opening every paragraph.** Moreover, Furthermore, That said, Additionally, Importantly. Real writing flows without them.
- **Bold word, colon, explanation bullets.** "Innovation: explanation. Scalability: explanation." A pure tell.
- **Corporate therapist voice.** "A powerful opportunity to lean into our strengths and foster a culture of accountability." Translation: nothing.
- **The neat little bow at the end.** "Ultimately, the goal is to build a more resilient organization." If the conclusion could close any memo on earth, it is filler.
- **The says-everything-means-nothing paragraph.** Reads as smart, then you cannot summarize it. If you cannot state a paragraph's point in one plain sentence, delete it.
- **Staccato for drama.** One- and two-word sentences. Keep a minimum of three to four words.
- **Tricolon ending in an adjective.** "Bold, brave and game-changing."
- **Forced parallelism.** Sentences that match grammatically when the ideas underneath do not actually parallel.
- **A three-item list with no progression.** "It's strategic, intentional and impactful."

## Shapes that pass a banned-word list

None of these is a banned word. Each one is a shape, and each makes a post or reply sound machine-made.

| Tell | Example | What a person would say |
|---|---|---|
| Compressed noun stack: three or four nouns jammed together | "My reply bot's tag synced in as his status" | "The AI that handles my replies tags anything it can't answer, and the tag was overwriting his status" |
| Engineer's maxim as the punchline | "Last write wins." | Say what happened: "the last thing to update the record is the one that sticks" |
| Abstract noun doing the verb's job | "14 more people lost their status the same way" | "I checked the rest. 14 more people had the same problem" |
| Label, colon, telegram: a label, a colon, then a recipe with the verbs cut | "The fix: tag first, write the status after, check every run." | "So now the tag goes on first and the real status gets written after it" |
| Callback words pointing at nothing: *again, the same way, that one* when the reader never saw the first time | "The fix, again" | Drop it, or say the thing |
| Inverted reveal: short setup, then a flipped fragment | "The agent isn't the hard part anymore. Where the agent works is." | "The hard part now is the data the agent works from" |
| "X is where the value is" or "What matters is" | "The trigger is where the value is." | Say who does what |
| Imperative maxim opener: a two-line commandment like a poster | "Record once a day. Build every other piece from that recording." | "I record one video a day and build everything else from it" |
| Echo opener in replies: repeat the commenter's word, then pivot | "Brutal is the word, which is why..." | Answer them: "Yeah, moving a CRM is painful, even when the new one is better" |
| Same skeleton in every reply: echo, a "since" clause, a fact, then a question with their company name in it | 20 of 30 replies | Mix it up. Some replies are one line. Some end on a statement |
| Their company name pulled from their headline | "At Acme, where do agency accounts stall?" | Name their company only if they named it in the comment |
| "Since" as the all-purpose joint | "since an AI that counts one human three times gives three wrong answers" | "because" |
| Feature dump in a reply | A long list of what the product logs | One plain sentence about what you do, in your own words |
| Stock idioms: *earns its keep, a clean split, quietly breaks, the cheapest way to find out, makes so much sense* | | The writer's own idioms only |
| Every reply ends in a question | 28 of 30 | Ask when you want the answer. Otherwise end on the point |
| Too tidy: no "I", no feeling, no story, every sentence balanced | | First person, what happened, how it felt, in the order it happened |

## The talk test

Read the line out loud as the writer on a call with a friend. If they would never say it in those words, rewrite it in words they would use. A sentence said out loud almost never has a colon, a noun stack or a maxim in it.

## What produces slop in the first place

- Do not paste a topic and ask for the finished piece. The bullets you would have sent are worth more than the draft that comes back.
- Dictate the first draft. Talk the idea through out loud. The transcript already sounds human because a human said it.
- Use the model on your draft, not instead of it: tighten, find vague parts, surface contradictions, suggest a specific word where a generic one sits.
