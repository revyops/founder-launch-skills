# Register by surface, and writing in someone's own voice

## Same voice, different setting

| | Website | Marketing video | Personal video | Cold email | LinkedIn post | LinkedIn DM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Read or heard | read | heard | heard | read | read | read |
| Who started it | they did | they did | you did | you did | they did | you did |
| Length | 40-120 words per block | seconds | 60-120 seconds | 70-90 words | 100-250 words | under 50 words |
| Technical ceiling | high | low | medium | low | medium | low |
| Who speaks | we | I | I | I | I | I |

### Heard versus read

Spoken copy needs shorter sentences, deliberate repetition, and tolerates fragments that look sloppy on a page. Written copy can carry a number-dense line or a table that would be unintelligible aloud. A video script should fail a proofread and pass a read-aloud. If a line would not survive being said on camera, it fails, however it looks written down.

### Invited versus interrupting

On a website the reader came to you, so you can open with a claim. In a cold email or DM you interrupted them, so the first line has to earn the second. Same voice, opposite opening move.

## What does not transfer from cold email advice to a web page

- "Questions beat statements." True when you interrupted someone. Wrong on a landing page, where a confident claim is the point.
- "No bullets or fragment lists." A cold email has no cards, headers or whitespace. A web page is built from them.
- "Connectors everywhere." *Which means, so, because* remove friction in a dense block of prose. On a page with headers and cards, the structure already does that work, and connectors slow scanning. Apply them to paragraphs. Leave cards alone.

## Opt-in pages and forms

- Put Terms and Privacy links only in a quiet footer at the very bottom of the page: small grey text, no bold, no colour, no underline, well away from every headline, form, button and calendar. Hide them on phones while the keyboard is open.
- Never put them under a button or form, and never write a line beside a call to action such as "By continuing, you agree". People click those links and stop. Each one adds doubt and costs sign-ups.
- A form ends at its button.

## Content posts (LinkedIn, video scripts)

Every post is a hook, a reason to keep reading, and a reward. Build it as a list, steps or a story.

- **Hook:** the first one or two lines, before the "see more" cut. A result with a number, a mistake, a surprising fact, or a specific "I" statement. State the stakes.
- **Retain:** each line earns the next. Short paragraphs. One idea per line group.
- **Reward:** the payoff the hook promised, with a real detail the reader can use.
- A body link lowers reach on LinkedIn. Say the link is in the comments, and put it in the first comment.

## Writing in a person's own voice (posts, replies, DMs)

Anything published under a person's name should sound like them talking, minus the filler. Brand copy and personal voice are different registers.

**Method**

1. Start from their own words on the subject: a transcript, a voice memo, a message they sent you. Clean up a little. Do not rewrite into neater sentences.
2. Lead with "I" and what happened, in the order it happened, in the past tense, with the number.
3. Let *because* and *so* carry the logic. Avoid *since* and colons doing the work.
4. Explain their own jargon in the next breath.
5. Say how it felt if it mattered.
6. Use their idioms, never invented ones.
7. Run the talk test from `ai-tells.md`.

**Replies**

- First name, then straight to the answer. No echo of their words, no "Great point".
- Often one or two sentences. Many end on a statement. Ask a question only when you want the answer.
- Disagree plainly, and say why from something that happened to the writer.
- A cheer or a thank-you can be one short line.
- Do not name the commenter's company unless they named it.
- Vary the shape. Twenty replies with the same skeleton read as a bot.

**Relaxations for a person's own voice only.** If they naturally open with "This is" or "So" when they speak, keep it in their posts and replies. Still banned: "Here's why:" as a setup line, rhetorical questions, em dashes, coined phrases and every tell in `ai-tells.md`.

**Spoken register is not brand register.** A person who dictates will say *kind of, sort of, so to speak*, interrupt themselves and hedge before committing. None of that belongs on a web page. Capture how the brand sounds, not how the founder talks on a call. Their own posts and replies are the opposite case.

## The trait everything else sits on

A good writer will not say what they cannot back. Before writing any claim, ask whether the person could defend it on a call with a skeptical buyer. If not, it does not go in. Refusals ("We will not claim X") are often more credible than boasts, and "things that stop happening" ("You stop finding out late") state value without accusing the reader.

## Product and documentation copy

Anything a customer will act on needs more care than marketing copy.

- Check capability claims against the real product before writing them.
- Separate what the product can do from what is switched on.
- Never overstate a limit, and never hide one.
- Name the silent failure: what happens when the thing goes wrong and nobody is told.
- Define terms where they first appear.

## Paid social ads

- The headline is the clear offer, named like a product.
- Punchy beats safe.
- Keep guarantee terms out of the ad.
- Name the people ("sales reps", not "them").
- One buyer callout that matches the image.
- Ask a question only when the buyer cannot answer without the product.
