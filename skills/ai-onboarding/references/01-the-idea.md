# The idea

## The one thing to understand

AI is brilliant when it reads clean, complete, current information. It makes things up when it reads messy, old or missing information. Almost everything else in this guide follows from that.

So the real work is not clever prompts. The work is storing what your business knows in a form the AI can read, keeping it clean, and adding to it every time you learn something.

## A picture to hold on to

Think of Claude as a brilliant new hire who forgets everything overnight.

- On day one it knows a lot about the world and nothing about your business.
- You hand it the company handbook and its own notebook (your vault, a folder of notes on your computer). It reads them every morning, so nothing has to be explained twice.
- You give it standing instructions for recurring jobs (skills): short notes such as "how we write emails" or "how we build reports".
- You give it keys to the building (connectors): the links that let Claude log in to your CRM, help desk, ad accounts and chat tool.
- You give it a clean customer file. If the file holds three copies of one customer with different phone numbers, the new hire will confidently call the wrong one.

A clean handbook, a clean customer file and good standing instructions give you a superb employee. Leave one out and you get confident nonsense.

## The layers

| Layer | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Notes | What you know and how you do things: methods, decisions and why, lessons, meeting notes, research |
| Customer data | One clean record per person, kept in your CRM and read live, not pasted into notes |
| Skills | Reusable rules for recurring work |
| Connectors | Live access to your tools |

A "knowledge graph" sounds technical. It is only a set of plain notes that link to each other, so the AI can follow a thread from one idea to the next.

## No advice without context

Before you ask Claude for an opinion, give it what it needs to have one. The order that works:

1. **Give it the context.** Connect the tools, have it read the history (tickets, chat channels, meeting transcripts, reports), and write a summary of where things stand into the vault.
2. **Set the rules.** Tell it how you want things done, and have it save those rules as skills.
3. **Build an outside baseline.** Ask it to research what recognised experts say, cite every source, and save the result.
4. **Add your judgement.** Now talk about your goals. Disagree with it. Push back.
5. **Save what you learned**, so the next conversation starts from here.

## What the human is still for

Repetitive, one-off tasks will mostly go to AI. People with good taste, good questions and clear opinions become many times more productive.

AI can write an ad in seconds. You still have to know what a good ad looks like and say why this one is not it. Your job becomes:

- **Deciding the aim.** What are we trying to accomplish, and what is the biggest thing in the way?
- **Judging the output.** Knowing what good looks like and saying so.
- **Unblocking it.** Signing in, granting access, answering its questions, pressing the final button.
- **Keeping the knowledge clean**, so tomorrow's work is better than today's.

You become a product manager for a team of very fast assistants. Focus matters more than ever: pointed at the wrong problem, AI gets you to the wrong place faster.

## Be a skeptic who uses it anyway

AI does make things up. The answer is to check it, not to avoid it. See `06-checking-the-work.md`.
