# Choosing your first use case

Starting with AI has the same trap as building a product: scope creeps until nobody remembers why you started. AI makes it worse, because every new idea takes five minutes to begin. Pick one thing, finish it, then pick the next.

## Start from the aim

Answer two questions and write the answers into your vault:

1. What are you trying to accomplish in the next 90 days? One sentence.
2. What is the biggest thing in the way? Time, a missing report, a person you cannot hire, a backlog.

Put AI on the constraint. Speeding up something that is not in the way feels productive and changes nothing.

## Score your candidates

List the tasks you or your team repeat every week. Score each one from 1 to 5:

| Question | 5 means |
|---|---|
| Hours it takes each week | Many hours, often across several people |
| Is it on the constraint? | It is the thing in the way |
| Can Claude reach the data? | There is a connector, or the data is already in files |
| How bad is a mistake? | A mistake is cheap and easy to spot. Reports and drafts score 5. Anything sent to customers or spending money scores low |
| Will you know if it worked? | There is a number or a clear before and after |

Start with the highest total. Prefer something that reads and reports over something that sends and spends. Once you trust it, move up to work that acts.

## First use cases that work well

| Use case | What Claude does | Starting connectors |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly performance report | Pulls numbers from your tools, builds a readable report, drafts a summary for you to approve | Analytics, ad accounts, CRM, chat |
| Support triage | Reads past tickets, sorts them, drafts replies from your past answers, sends a daily summary | Help desk, chat, CRM |
| Call coaching | Reads past coaching sessions to learn your standards, then reviews each day's calls against them | Meeting recorder, chat |
| Account history | Reads every email, call and note on your biggest accounts and writes up what was tried and where things stand | Email, CRM, meeting recorder |
| Writing in your voice | Reads your past writing and builds rules so drafts sound like you | Your sent email or documents |
| Research baseline | Gathers what recognised experts say on a subject, cites every source, saves it | None; it searches the web |

## An example of how one grows

A support person calls in sick without warning. Covering the gap raises a bigger question: does this role need to be done by hand? It becomes a four-hour session:

1. Connect the help desk.
2. Read the past year of tickets and sort them by type.
3. Research how good support teams handle each type.
4. Save the categories, tone and standard answers in the vault.
5. Build a daily support report that runs on its own and posts to chat.
6. Draft replies for a person to approve, and send only the hard cases to them.

One afternoon, one constraint, one working system. It improves every day as the vault fills.

## What to avoid at the start

- Five projects at once. You will finish none.
- Anything where a mistake is public or expensive, until you trust it.
- A use case whose data you cannot connect. You will spend the session copying and pasting.
