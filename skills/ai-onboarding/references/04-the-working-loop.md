# The working loop

Most of the day is talking to Claude, sending it off to work, checking what comes back, and correcting it. At first you have one conversation open. Within a few weeks you may have six or seven running at once.

## How to talk to it

**Speak to it like the smartest person you know, and dump everything.** Use dictation, talk for two or three minutes, and include the goal, the background, the edge cases, what you have tried and what worries you. Do not tidy it first. Claude sorts it out.

**Ask for its view before you decide.** End almost every brain dump with "What do you think the right path is? Give me options." It comes back with a plan and usually two or three choices.

**Make it the expert, every time.** Tell it once, in your standing instructions, that in whatever field you are working it should answer as a senior expert: what experts and the best companies do, what you should do and why, then "what would you like to do?" A bare question back to you with no view attached means it skipped that step. Say so and it will fix the habit.

**Answer its questions the fast way.** When it asks five questions, read each aloud and say your answer straight after it. No need to format anything.

**Pick, and say go.** "Go with option B. What do you need from me to start?"

### Prompts that help

- "What do you think? What would you do?"
- "Give me three options with the trade-offs."
- "Explain this to me like I'm five." If the explanation is good, follow with "save that explanation so you explain it that way next time."
- "What do you need from me?"
- "Before you build anything, show me the plan."

## The rhythm of a working day

1. Start a piece of work with a brain dump in its own chat.
2. Agree the plan, then let it run. A big job takes five minutes or more.
3. Switch to another chat while it works.
4. Come back, check the result, correct it. "Not like that. I meant this." Expect several rounds on anything new.
5. When it is right, have it saved as a skill or a vault note (see `05-teaching-it-to-remember.md`).

### You become the project manager

Claude will send you on errands: sign in here, get me this file, approve this access, decide this. With several chats going you will lose track. Two fixes:

- Ask for a running to-do list: "Keep a list in the vault of everything you are waiting on me for, across all our work, and show it to me when I ask."
- Ask for a daily summary of every scheduled job and what each one needs from you.

## Reports and dashboards

Ask for a report and you get a wall of text. A good report takes iteration: "put it in tabs", "make the rows expand into a trend chart", "show the team total."

Once you like a format, make it the standard:

> "Look at these reports. I like the layout and the look. Write a skill so every report you build from now on follows the same format, and save the standard in the vault."

## Scheduled jobs

You do not need to touch the scheduling screen. Describe the schedule in plain words:

> "Pull this report every week. Run it at 2am Monday. Put it in draft, send it to me in a direct message, and when I approve it, post it to the marketing channel."

- Jobs on your computer only run while it is awake. If one is marked skipped, that is usually why. Open it and press Run now.
- Cloud jobs run on remote servers and do not need your computer, but cannot reach files or tools that only exist on your machine. Ask Claude which kind suits the job.

## Working inside other tools

With a connector or the browser, Claude can work in your tools directly: "open the ad account, show me how the first campaign did, build four new ads in the same set." Check what it built before it goes live, and press the final launch button yourself. It will get stuck on some settings it cannot reach, so stay nearby while it works inside a live system.
