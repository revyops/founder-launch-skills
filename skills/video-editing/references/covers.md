# Covers: the visual hook in still form

Every video, on every channel that has a cover control (LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook), goes out with a cover made for it. A video with no cover is not ready to post. Without one, the platform picks a frame, often the first. An animation's first frame is an empty background. A talking head's is often mid-blink. One how-to animation went up on LinkedIn as a blank white screen that way. Some platforms cannot change a cover after posting, so get it right first.

## What every cover has

- **The promise in big words.** Two to six words that say what the viewer gets: the hook title or the outcome. The accent colour goes on the one key word. This is the largest thing on the cover.
- **At most one line under it** that names the payoff or the twist.
- **One picture of the payoff:** the finished diagram, the result screen, the before and after, or the face with a clear expression. Never an empty or half-built frame. Never an animation's first frame. Build the cover as its own still from the film's finished frame.
- **The face,** for a founder or creator brand, small enough that the words stay the main thing. Eyes open.
- **The brand's video look:** background, colours and font.
- **Carry the promise, not just the series name.** A series name and day number alone is boring. Add the stake in big words and what it is for.

## Safe box for a 9:16 cover

Platforms crop a tall cover. A profile grid shows the 3:4 centre (1080x1440, y 240 to 1680). A feed shows 4:5 (y 285 to 1635). The grid lays a view count over the bottom left and a pin over the top right. So every word, number, arrow and the face sit inside **x 110 to 970, y 340 to 1500** of the 1080x1920 cover. Outside it is background only. One cover lost its words on the grid this way.

- On a face cover, the title sits on the hat or forehead, big, top at y 400 or lower, with the underline just above the eyebrows. Never floating in the empty space above the head. Move the face up rather than adding ceiling.
- For a 16:9 cover keep 80 px or more from each edge.
- A poster for a video that plays inside a page that has its own title and buttons: before play, the page may cover the bottom third and the middle. Keep labels beside the head and above about 60% of the height (y 1150 or less of 1920). Check on a phone-width screenshot of the live page.

## Checks

1. Scale it to about 420 px wide (a phone feed). Every word of the promise is readable and the picture still says what it is.
2. Can someone say what the video teaches from the cover alone? If not, rewrite the words.
3. Crop it to the grid (3:4) and feed (4:5) shapes and look. Anything cut means move or shrink it and check again.
4. Nothing important near the edges. A drawn safe-box command is in `commands.md`.
5. Make the cover with the video, before the review, one for every channel. A long video that also goes to LinkedIn uses its YouTube thumbnail there, never a frame LinkedIn picks.
6. After posting, open the live post and confirm the feed shows the cover.
