# Framing, zoom, captions and titles for a talking-head Short

Rule: frame a Short the way top-performing Shorts are framed, because viewers are used to that. Do not invent your own placement. The numbers below were measured from 33 top talking-head Shorts (about plus or minus 0.02). All numbers are fractions of the 9:16 frame: y from the top, x from the left. Face width is ear to ear as a share of the frame width.

## 1. Where the head goes (all zoom levels)

| What | Hard limit | Usual range | Median |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top of head | at most 0.13 down (hair may be cropped) | 0.01 to 0.13 | 0.06 |
| Eye line | 0.18 to 0.47 | 0.25 to 0.43 | 0.31 |
| Chin | 0.41 to 0.80 | 0.42 to 0.75 | 0.51 |
| Face centre (x) | 0.33 to 0.55 | 0.40 to 0.52 | 0.45 |
| Face width | 0.27 to 0.95 | 0.40 to 0.94 | 0.60 |
| Caption line | 0.50 to 0.83 | 0.52 to 0.68 | 0.58 |

In plain words:

- **No headroom.** The top of the head is at the top edge or just under it. Zooming in crops the hair. It never adds ceiling.
- **Eyes sit on or a little under the top-third line.** Never in the lower half.
- **The face stays in the middle strip.** Centred when talking to camera. A little left of centre (0.40 to 0.45) when looking off to one side, with the space on the side they look toward.
- **Zoom pivots at the top of the head.** As the shot gets closer, the head grows downward: the eyes drop from about 0.29 to 0.37 and the chin from 0.50 to 0.65.
- **Never move the picture down to make room above the head.** The head then sits too low.

## 2. Zoom levels

| Size | Face width | Eye line | Chin | Share of top Shorts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tight | 0.70 to 0.95 | 0.36 | 0.65 | 45% |
| Medium | 0.42 to 0.70 | 0.29 | 0.50 | 36% |
| Wide | 0.27 to 0.42 | 0.29 | 0.46 | 18% |

- Use tight and medium. Wide is rare.
- **Talking to camera goes tight.** The median face width for camera-facing shots is 0.90. Cut to medium for a change of size.
- **Jump cuts change size** between tight and medium (about a third bigger or smaller). Never two pieces the same size in a row.
- **Quality:** from a 4K vertical phone file, a tight shot (face about 0.78) needs a crop about 1,000 px wide. Never go below a 900 px crop. A 2x upscale of a small crop looks soft.
- **Move the front shot gently:** a slow push-in, then a slow pull-out. Eyes about 0.37 on a push-in and 0.31 on a pull-out. Zoom (on a 2160x3840 file) roughly 2.15 to 2.25 in and 1.70 to 1.62 out. Put a 6-frame 1.07 punch on each change of angle.
- **Follow a moving face.** When the speaker leans, find the face about every 0.25 s, smooth it lightly, and move the crop with it. One fixed crop for a whole shot lets a lean drift to the edge.

## 3. Captions

- One fixed line, 55 to 61% down (57.5% is y 1104 of 1920), whatever the zoom. On a tight shot it sits on the lower lip, beard and chin. Covering the mouth there is normal. Never put it above the nose.
- 1 to 3 words, white, bold sans, soft dark shadow, about 3 to 4% of the frame tall (Montserrat ExtraBold at about 76 px on 1080x1920). One key word may take an accent colour.
- Never wider than x 200 to 880 (680 px of text, centred). The like, comment and share column on the right starts near y 1040. A group that would run wider splits into shorter groups.
- Earlier tests put captions at the chin line about 62% down, or at y 1440. y 1440 is inside the bottom area the app covers. Do not use it.

## 4. The title on screen

- The usual look is a white rounded box with bold black text in two lines, 57 to 71% down, right under the caption line. That was the biggest group in the study. A top banner or a box on the forehead was rare.
- A series opener ("Series name / Day n") is a title box, big and over the mouth (Montserrat ExtraBold about 100 px, centre y about 1080), on screen from frame 1, with the captions paused. It is gone by about 1 second. Never a small label at the top, where the app bar covers it.
- Right after the hook line, a name card in the same spot for about 1 second ("Name", "Founder of" and a logo) so viewers know who is talking. Captions pause.
- The box clears the caption line by about 40 px. Two texts never share the caption line.
- A burned-in hook title in a video (not a cover): on the forehead, top at y 400 or lower, x 110 to 970, underline above the brows. Not on the hairline and not above the head. Platforms' top bars cut that area off. Lines clearly apart, no touching letters.
- Never over eyes, nose or mouth when the title stays on for long, except the opener box, which is gone in about a second and is meant to be over the mouth.
- Split screen (guest on top, speaker below): keep the title and label off the guest's whole panel and off the speaker from the eyebrows down. Look at a still from the title seconds.

## 5. Safe zones (all in 1080x1920)

- Top 13% (about y 250) and bottom 25% (about y 1440 down) belong to the app. Sides: 12% each, because the buttons sit on the right.
- Every word, card, label and arrow inside x 110 to 970. Nothing within 110 px of a side edge. Text on screen sits in y 250 to 1440. Inside panels, nothing above y 290.
- Overlay cards: centred, main line about 64 px, at least 430 px wide, fade in (no slide from the side). They never sit over the eyes (about y 700 to 1250). End a card at a cut if the next shot would put it over the face.
- The name chip sits in the lower-middle band under the captions, about 1.3 times the size of a plain label.
- If a video will play on your own page under the page's bars, measure what the page covers. One phone page covered the top 270 px and the bottom third, so tags had to stay in y 300 to 1100.
- Landscape (16:9): no crop. YouTube's controls cover about the bottom 10%. A title can sit beside the speaker. Captions can use 2 lines and about 60 px on a 1080-high frame. Longer pieces allow gentler pause trimming.

## 6. Picture (set when filming, not in the edit)

| What | What top Shorts do | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Colour | Natural, warm skin | Pass it through. Never grade |
| Light | One key light from the side, so one cheek is darker; face brighter than the room | Key at about 45 degrees to one side |
| Background | Darker than the face, soft, often coloured | One warm or coloured light behind; room darker than the face |
| Focus | Eyes sharp, background blurred | Film further back on the 2x or 3x lens |
| Lens look | Long lens: flat face, no big nose | 2x (about 52 mm) or 3x. Never 1x up close |
| Angle | Lens at eye level; most look slightly off camera | A diary looks straight in. A story can look three-quarters off |

**Never grade, apply a LUT, or tone-map footage yourself.** A "fix" made one speaker look like a different person. Pass colour through. iPhone HDR is converted to standard colour only with Apple's own render (the one QuickTime shows), and a frame from your file should differ from Apple's frame by less than 3 of 255. If a camera looks off in its own file, that is a filming fix (white balance), not an edit fix. Use lanczos scaling, low zoom and high-quality intermediate files (x264 crf 12, not a hardware encoder).

## 7. Frame gate before every send

Script a gate that samples one frame a second, finds the face (OpenCV or MediaPipe), and fails any frame outside the hard limits in section 1 (eye line 0.20 to 0.47, face centre 0.33 to 0.57, face width inside the measured range). Skip the seconds when a panel, opener, name card or callout covers the face on purpose, and judge those by eye. The goal is zero fails. Then look at one phone-size still of every side shot and every overlay.
