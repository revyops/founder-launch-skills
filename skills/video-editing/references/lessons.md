# Lessons: story, hooks, cutting, checks and common mistakes

Every rule here came from real edits that a speaker reviewed. Read the part you need before you plan the cut. Placement numbers are in `framing.md`. Multi-camera is in `multicam.md`. Panels and big words are in `panels.md`. Covers are in `covers.md`.

## 1. Story: five parts, mapped before the first cut

Every video needs these, in this order:

1. **Hook.** The viewer's pain in their own words, or a claim with a number or a reversal. It is usually said in the middle of the raw take, after the speaker warmed up. Lift it to the front. Short: 3 to 5 seconds. Long: 5 to 10.
2. **Promise.** What the viewer gets by staying. Short: one line. Long: finish the point before moving on.
3. **Credibility.** Why this speaker. One fixed line of facts (years, results), the same words every video. In a Short it is usually skipped. In a long video it goes after the promise is finished and right before the demo, never in the middle of the argument.
4. **Fulfilment.** Deliver exactly what the promise said, in an order a viewer can follow. Cut sections that do not pay it off, even good ones. Keep them for another video.
5. **Call to action.** One ask that fits the funnel stage. Mid-video and at the end for a long video. Last 5 seconds for a Short.

**Before any cut, map the footage to these parts.** Write the source times and the exact words for each part in a spec file. If a part is missing or weak, say which pickup take would fix it:

- Hook missing: look for the strongest line anywhere in the take (often 15 to 60 seconds in). If there is none, ask for a one-breath pickup straight to the lens.
- Promise missing: the opening claim plus a designed graphic can carry it. If the payoff is never said, ask for a pickup line.
- Credibility missing: reuse the speaker's approved take if one exists. Otherwise ask them to film it.
- Call to action missing: a lower third and an end card carry it. Never invent spoken words.

A two-minute pickup take beats an hour of editing around a gap. Film the credibility line, a clean hook and an end card once, and reuse them.

## 2. Hooks and the shape of a Short

How the feed works: it tests every Short on a small group first. If that group stays, the Short goes to more people. Two numbers matter. One is how many stayed past the first 2 to 3 seconds (aim for 70% or more). The other is the average share watched (aim for 80% or more). Shorter makes both easier. Treat these as guides. They are creator and analyst numbers, not platform promises.

**The hook has one job:** the stranger knows what it is about and wants the next line.

- Name the topic in the first 1 to 2 seconds. No warm-up. Cut every line before the one that names the topic.
- Say it plainly. Fifth-grade words, active voice. If the line can mean two things, rewrite it.
- Make it about the viewer. The story can say "I", but the stake must be one the viewer shares.
- Open one question and hold the answer. Never give the answer in the hook or the first frame. Close it at the end.
- Use contrast from real facts: what people believe against what happened.
- Then foreshadow in one line what they will see by the end.
- Write five hooks, keep one. Test it: would it work as the title of a long video?
- A hook needs a call-out to the viewer and a value promise, both.
- Banned as opening words: a label such as "Episode N of...", vague hype ("You won't believe this"), and a question nobody asked.
- Under 8 words for the on-screen hook text, first person, with stakes ("doomed before I even began"), not just a fact. It must make sense with the sound off.
- The spoken hook is said once. A teaser hook comes from later in the clip. If the best line already opens the clip, use no separate hook. Otherwise the line plays twice in ten seconds.
- A lifted hook runs straight into the next line with no repeated words at the join. Record the hook and the joining line in one take if you can. ("Your CRM thinks Maria is three people. It knows her by her email." Not "...three people. Your CRM knows Maria by her email.")

**The middle**

- One story per Short. Pick the one moment that changed something.
- Join beats with "but" and "so", never "and then".
- A new picture every 2 to 4 seconds: a camera change, a push-in, B-roll, a number card. No dead air.
- Give a reason to stay: a number or result held back, or "three things happened" so they know where they are.

**The end**

- Write the last line before filming. End the moment the payoff lands. No sign-off, no recap, no "follow for more".
- No long end card on a Short or Reel. Put the ask in the caption or first comment. Long end cards stay on sites and platforms where watch time works differently.
- A loop is good when it is natural: the last line runs into the first line.
- Hold the last frame 0.4 s and end the audio exactly with the video. If the audio runs even 0.07 s longer, phones show black.
- Never fade to black. End on the last line plus a short hold, or on the brand's own end screen (about 7 s for a long video). The end screen should match the page it sends people to.
- A call to action must fit every platform the video goes to. Cut "comment below" from a video that also goes on YouTube. A Short that feeds a long video ends on a neutral line such as "Full video: tap the link below", and the Related video link is set at upload.
- **Length.** Aim for 30 to 60 seconds. Up to about 100 seconds is fine when the story needs it. Coherence beats a length target. YouTube Shorts and Reels stop at 3:00. For a longer vertical video, keep the full cut for your site and LinkedIn, and make a separate cut of 2:58 or less for Shorts and Reels. Cut whole passages, never speed. Cut general talk first, then repeats, then sign-offs. Never post the long one as a regular video to dodge the limit.
- **Repurposing means rebuilding.** A cut from an old film is rebuilt for the new format: its own hook, story, point and call to action, composed natively at 1080x1920. Never a cropped, panned or letterboxed landscape render. Storyboard first and review at phone size.

**Be honest.** Authentic means the events and words are real. It does not mean unedited. The hook only puts the truest, most interesting line first. Never invent a number, a feeling or a cliffhanger. Polish the edit, not the person. A low number said plainly can be the hook.

**How-to films (animations and explainers).** Open on a hook card in the first 2 to 3 seconds. Line one says what it shows in the words people search for ("How to automate X"), never a coined label. Line two is the twist. Show where the data comes from before the result. Tell it in story order. A ranked list must look ranked: rank numbers, scores, bars, sorted, and shown being sorted.

## 3. Cutting rules that are easy to miss

- **Find restarts and double takes from the audio, not the transcript.** A long Whisper transcript smooths over restarts. Split the audio into islands at each pause, read every island, and keep only the last clean take of any line said twice.
- **Doubled words at a join** ("and again, and again I can"): cut to one.
- **"So" and "Now" opening a line** add nothing: cut. Keep one when it runs straight into the next word and cutting it clips that word (a "So" before "I" can turn into "Though").
- **Two words can be fused in the audio** ("So that's"). If cutting the first changes the second, keep both.
- **Word times can be late by up to 0.8 s.** Never place a cut from word times alone. Use the quiet after the word's last sound. Word ends like "s" are quiet and easy to clip. Check the join by listening or by transcribing a short window of it.
- **Fillers get their own pass.** Re-transcribe with the filler prompt in chunks of about 25 seconds, because Whisper drops the prompt after its first 30-second window. Cut only the fillers the audio energy confirms. The prompt also invents some.
- **Cut a filler as a burst of sound:** from the quiet before it to the quiet after it. Whisper's timing for "the" and "uh" can be 0.3 s early.
- **Do not cut a held vowel** ("today,") if the pause is part of the word.
- **Joins.** A quarter-second beat is the default. For a founder who filmed lines one at a time, make it sound like one person talking: trim each line to its own sound (about 0.06 s after the last sound, about 0.02 s before a first sound that runs on from the last, 0.08 s otherwise), shrink pauses inside a line over 0.24 s to 0.14 s, and keep every join at 0.20 s or less. A 2-second gap between lines sounds wrong. Measure the gaps on the cut voice before you render. Any join over 0.25 s is a fail.
- **Fewer cuts read better.** About 25 pieces in 100 seconds felt fine. 35 felt choppy. Pieces under 1.5 s from pause trimming should keep the pause instead.
- **Mask jump cuts** with a punch-in: a 6 to 10% scale change on the join frame, or on every other piece. A jump cut inside a held layout looks like a glitch.
- **Fades.** Use 8 to 10 ms audio fades at each join and 15 ms at the end. Where speech runs on with no pause, cut at the softest point and fade over about 60 ms.
- **Local noise.** Judge "quiet" against the background noise near the cut, not the whole clip. Outdoor clips have wind and traffic.
- **Whisper invents lines after the video ends** ("There we go..."). Ignore them.
- **Transcribe joins in short windows (about 6 s).** Long windows make the transcriber invent words.
- **Never leave a card or text over a silent, staring face.** Keep the statement up over the start of the next line instead.
- **Swears:** bleep every one the output transcript finds, not only the ones you remember.
- **A final pass never re-cuts an approved join.** Change only what the notes name, plus edges that truly clip a word. A filler inside an approved line stays. Keep the approved cut list and diff the new one against it before you render. Every changed piece must trace to a note.
- **Render pieces with audio exactly as long as video**, or captions drift by up to 0.7 s over 35 joins.
- **Pieces split from one take share one loudness gain.** Different takes are matched to the same loudness before joining.

## 4. Captions: extra rules

- Captions go on one fixed line, whatever the zoom (see `framing.md`). The face moves under the caption, not the caption around the face.
- No wider than x 200 to 880 on a 1080-wide frame. The right edge has the app's buttons.
- Spell names right. Check caption line one against the script, because Whisper can write "launched" for "launch".
- Pause the captions while a title box, name card or full-screen panel is showing. Two texts never share the caption line. Captions can keep running under a small callout above the forehead.
- Show a group whole and dim the words not yet said (about 45%), lighting each word on its own start. No word ever sits alone. Never caption a big word twice.
- Timing: take each word's start from the transcript, then move it to where the audio actually gets loud. A caption 3 frames late feels wrong.
- Over a page or screen recording, use a solid contrasting box (white box and dark text on a dark page, dark box and white text on a light page). Never an accent colour.
- Libass sizes fonts by usWinAscent plus usWinDescent, not the em. Measure wrapping with the real font.

## 5. Common mistakes, and what stops each

| Mistake | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Restarts and repeats left in | Cut from the transcript | Find them from the audio islands |
| Clipped word endings | Cut on the voice | Cut in the quiet after the last sound, then listen to the join |
| 2-second gaps between lines | Kept the silence around lines filmed one at a time | Trim each line to its sound, joins 0.20 s or less |
| Head low, far from the lens | Placement by eye | Use the measured bounds in `framing.md` |
| Random words popping with a whoosh | Effect on every other word | A few big words only (`panels.md`) |
| Title above the head or on the hairline | Free space looks empty | Title on the forehead, top at y 400 or lower |
| Our own colour grade made skin look wrong | LUT on phone footage | Never grade. Pass colour through |
| Panels leave too fast | No hold rule | Hold about 1 s after the last item |
| Labels cut off at the top | App bar covers the top | Nothing above y 290 |
| A fact on screen that was never said | Filled from memory | Check every label against the transcript or the brief |
| Date or number given three ways | Facts changed in chat | Put facts once in the brief. If two disagree, ask before rendering |
| Used the wrong camera file | Did not check the file | Check length, size and sync strength |
| A hand over the side lens | Side shot picked without a check | Use a side angle only where the hands are down |
| Effect tested only on lit footage | Dark footage kills cut-outs | Refilm dark footage instead of rescuing it |
| Hand-patching one huge script | Edits piled up | Back up the script before each change and rebuild stills after each |
| A final pass chopped an approved line | Re-cut a passed join | Diff the new cut list against the approved one |
| Text clipped inside a card | No layout check | A still of every panel at its settled frame |

## 6. The review loop, and when to stop

Fast checks first, people last, and a fixed number of rounds. The speaker should never be the reviewer.

1. **Gate before any render (seconds).** Script it. Check from the cut plan: doubled words, "So" and "Now" openers, fillers, gaps inside a line, swears, the opening shot, B-roll ending on a full stop, boxes inside the safe band and big enough, the title at y 400 or lower, loud joins. Fix every fail. Listen to every warning.
2. **Phone-size stills before any render:** the opening, every side shot, every overlay. Fix what looks wrong here, not after a render.
3. **720p draft for a separate reviewer.** The reviewer never waits for the 4K. Give a fresh agent the draft, the brief, the cut list, the speaker's last notes, the checklist and the gate output. Ask it to answer each line pass or fail with the time, list what else the speaker would notice, and give a chance in percent that the speaker has no notes.
4. **Round 2:** fix every fail, re-run the gate, render a new draft, review again. Give the reviewer a "kept on purpose" list with a reason for each item, so it does not raise them again.
5. **Round 3 is a spot check only.** Check just what changed: a still at each changed shot, and the join transcript at each changed join. No new full review.
6. **Then the 4K, once:** frame count, a still at each changed join, a re-transcription around each changed join.
7. **Send one version.** Include the reviewer's percent, what was kept on purpose and why, and any open note in plain words (a filming fault, for example). Never two versions to compare. Never a fourth round.

The reviewer is good on what it can measure (limits, hold times, gaps, positions) and weak on "will the speaker like the design". Its percent falls as you add layers even when the speaker likes the result. Trust the written standard for taste. A bigger model is not the fix for speed. The loop is.

**Content words go into the brief.** Anything the speaker says in review that is content (labels, facts, steps) goes into the brief or card, not only into chat notes. A fresh agent cannot see chat notes. If a list panel has no words from the speaker, ask once before building it.

**Brief check.** Check the piece against its brief too: hook, offer, call to action.

## 7. Delivery

- Deliver the full-quality final and a small 720p preview under 30 MB. Say which is which.
- Upload the final untouched. Never re-encode, downscale or compress a video to fit an upload limit. LinkedIn takes 1080p files up to 5 GB, so a small file was a tool limit, not a platform limit. If the upload route fails, the post waits.
- Build media at the source resolution. A layer cut down to 1080p goes soft on every push-in.
- A 4K rebuild of an approved cut: change only the scale numbers, check the cut map is identical, and compare the 4K scaled to 1080p against the approved 1080p (PSNR 38 dB or more).
- Audio: voice about -14 LUFS, true peak below -1 dB. Voice -15 and master -14 for films.
- One music bed, low under the voice (about 19 to 24 dB under), never restarted or swapped mid-video. If you mix, use stems. Never butt two mixed clips together.
- Effects only on real events, at low volume. About one soft whoosh per panel or callout. Nothing per word.
- Never leave a gap, a held frame or a music restart between sections. The next line comes in within a normal sentence pause.
- Blur private data in footage with a keep-out box over any pop-up or menu you are explaining, so the blur never covers it.
- A video that plays inside a page's own bars (a swipe feed, a story-style player): measure what the page covers and keep burned-in tags inside what stays clear.
- Voiceover films: a human read beats an AI voice. Pauses go by punctuation (up to 0.22 s inside a sentence, 0.3 to 0.55 s between sentences). If the read is too long, cut words, never speed past about 4%.
